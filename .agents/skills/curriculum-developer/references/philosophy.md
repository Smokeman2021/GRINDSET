# Core Philosophy: Backward Design Framework

This section outlines a **Backward Design Framework** for educational planning, structured in three sequential stages:

**Stage 1** asks educators to clarify learning objectives by determining "What should learners know, understand, and be able to do?"

**Stage 2** requires establishing assessment methods, addressing "How will we know if learners achieved results?"

**Stage 3** involves creating instructional activities that "equip learners" with necessary skills and knowledge.

The framework emphasizes that "Backward design prevents the common mistake of 'activity-oriented' teaching without clear outcomes."

Five guiding principles anchor this approach:
- Prioritize learner needs over subject matter
- Align objectives, assessments, and activities cohesively
- Build accessibility into initial design
- Pursue deep understanding rather than broad coverage
- Use evidence from learners to refine instruction

This methodology addresses a fundamental problem in education: teaching activities disconnected from measurable outcomes.
